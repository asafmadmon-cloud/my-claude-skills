# Buffett Investment Criteria Reference

## Buffett's Core Philosophy
"It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."
— Warren Buffett, 1989 Berkshire Hathaway Letter

The ideal Buffett investment: a **Wide Moat business** with **honest and capable management** that can be purchased at a **significant discount to intrinsic value** and **held forever**.

---

## The 4-Filter System (Must Pass All)

### Filter 1: Enduring Competitive Advantage (MOAT)
The business must have a durable competitive advantage that protects future cash flows.

**MOAT Types (score each 0-5, total max 25):**
| Type | What It Means | Key Test |
|------|--------------|---------|
| Switching Costs | Customers can't easily leave | Would it cost $X to switch? |
| Network Effect | Value grows with more users | Does each new user make existing users richer? |
| Cost Advantage | Lowest cost producer | Can anyone undercut their price profitably? |
| Intangible Assets | Brand, patent, license | Can competitors replicate this? |
| Efficient Scale | Natural oligopoly | Would it be rational for a new entrant to compete? |

**Scoring:**
- 18-25: Wide Moat ✅ (Buffett-quality)
- 10-17: Narrow Moat ⚠️ (monitor)
- 0-9: No Moat ❌ (avoid)

**What Buffett avoids:** commodity businesses, airlines (pre-2016), businesses needing constant reinvention, technology-dependent businesses where the moat can evaporate overnight.

---

### Filter 2: Able and Trustworthy Management

**Signs of excellent management:**
- ROE consistently ≥ 15% over 5+ years (competence signal)
- Candid shareholder letters that discuss both successes AND failures honestly
- Capital allocation: rational buybacks when undervalued, dividends when no better use
- No excessive dilution (shares outstanding not growing > 2%/yr)
- Insider ownership ≥ 5% (skin in the game)
- CEO tenure > 5 years with track record

**Red flags:**
- ROE only high due to leverage (check D/E)
- Earnings manipulation signals (accruals ratio high, big "one-time" charges repeatedly)
- Stock-based compensation > 3% of net income
- CEO who overpromises and underdelivers consistently
- Acquisition-happy culture with goodwill write-downs

---

### Filter 3: Sensible Price (Margin of Safety)
Buffett requires a significant discount to intrinsic value as a cushion against being wrong.

**Minimum margin of safety: 20-25%**
**Buffett's sweet spot: 40%+ for highest conviction**

Two valuation methods:
1. **Owner Earnings DCF** — projects 10 years of Owner Earnings discounted at 10%
2. **Graham Number** — √(22.5 × EPS × BVPS) — conservative ceiling for defensive investor

If current price > intrinsic value → wait. Buffett waits years for the right price.

---

### Filter 4: Generates Free Cash Flow
The business must be self-funding and generate real cash for owners.

**Owner Earnings (Buffett's definition):**
`Net Income + Depreciation/Amortization − Capital Expenditures`

Why this matters: Net Income can be manipulated. Owner Earnings is the cash available to the business owner after maintaining competitive position.

**Tests:**
- Owner Earnings positive in 4 of 5 years = PASS
- Owner Earnings Yield > 6% (at 10% discount rate) = value zone
- CapEx / Depreciation < 1.5 = not CapEx-intensive (Buffett prefers asset-light)

---

## The Buffett "Circle of Competence" Test
Before any analysis, ask: "Do I understand this business well enough to predict its earnings in 10 years?"

If the answer is NO → do not invest regardless of price. This is not a failure; it's discipline.

Buffett famously avoided tech in the 1990s not because it was bad business, but because he couldn't predict winners with confidence.

---

## What Buffett Explicitly Avoids
- Commodity businesses with no pricing power
- Airlines (historically — high capex, labor issues, price competition)
- Businesses requiring constant technology reinvention
- Companies with complex, opaque financial structures
- Management that enriches itself at shareholders' expense
- Businesses with Debt/Equity > 1.0 (excessive leverage)
- Negative FCF for 2+ consecutive years

---

## The "Owner Mentality" Test
Buffett asks: "If I owned the whole business, would I be satisfied with this return?"

- Would the business generate enough cash to justify the purchase price?
- Is management honest about problems, not just celebrating wins?
- Would I want to own this forever, or am I speculating on price appreciation?

If the answer to any of these is "no" → HOLD or AVOID.

---

## Buffett on Diversification
"Diversification is protection against ignorance. It makes little sense if you know what you are doing."

For the **Satellite portfolio (Bucket B)**: Concentration in 5-10 high-conviction Wide Moat stocks is acceptable.
For the **Core portfolio (Bucket A)**: Diversification via ETFs with broad market exposure is exactly right for most investors. Buffett has repeatedly told retail investors to buy S&P 500 index funds.

---

## The "Newspaper Test" for Management
Imagine your actions printed on the front page of your local newspaper. Would you be embarrassed?

Apply this to management decisions: aggressive accounting, excessive compensation, misleading guidance = fail.
